# Visual Identity

## 1. Non-negotiable visual qualities

- Premium cinematography and careful scene composition
- Beautiful, *relevant* background selection
- Consistent color grading across all scenes of a project
- High-quality, realistic or intentionally stylized visuals
- Cinematic depth: controlled shadows, beautiful highlights, deep but detailed blacks

## 2. Backgrounds must match the narration — critical rule

The background always relates to what is being said **at that exact moment**. A
beautiful but unrelated background is a failure.

| Narration (example) | Required visual |
|---|---|
| "বাংলাদেশের গ্রামীণ জীবনে নদী গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ভূমিকা রাখে।" | Cinematic Bangladeshi river scene — boats, villages, natural movement |
| "মৌমাছি ফুল থেকে মধু সংগ্রহ করে।" | Detailed macro of a bee collecting nectar |
| "আল্লাহর সৃষ্টির এক বিস্ময়কর নিদর্শন হলো মহাবিশ্ব।" | Researched cosmic visualization with educational context |

**Matching procedure per scene:**
1. Understand the meaning of the narration segment.
2. Identify the exact visual subject.
3. Select or generate the appropriate background.
4. Match camera movement to the narration.
5. Add relevant motion graphics.
6. Place text so it never hides the important visual information.
7. Keep the studio's visual identity consistent.

Every scene must visually **explain or reinforce** the narration. The relevant subject
is the main focus; text and graphics add information.

## 3. Vertical-first composition

- Designed natively for 9:16 vertical viewing — never a cropped horizontal video.
- Respect mobile-safe areas for all critical text and subjects
  (see `VIDEO_TECHNICAL_STANDARDS.md`).
- Layer composition: background → subject → graphics → typography → signature.

## 4. Variety doctrine

Do not fill time with repeated imagery. Vary shot scale (wide/medium/close/macro),
camera language and graphic treatment while holding one consistent grade and identity.
